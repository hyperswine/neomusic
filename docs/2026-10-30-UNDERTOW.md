# Undertow: synths inside the track

Kind: original composition and blending study, 2026-10-30. Written to the
brief that Emberfall (2026-10-29) made obvious: synths used as foreground
stand-ins (a naked saw lead, a pure-synth chug) read as "on the nose" rather
than as part of the track. Undertow is the counter-study: a downtempo piece,
G minor, 92 bpm, 32 bars (110 s with the ritard and the hall), where recorded
instruments carry the music and every synthetic voice supports. Score:
[examples/neomusic/undertow.neomusic](../examples/neomusic/undertow.neomusic);
listen: [MP3](../examples/neomusic/audio/undertow.mp3).

Form: piano alone (1-4), pad and kick blooming under it (5-12), bass and
swung hats (9-12), strings with the lift (13-20), the tune on violins with
the halo doubling it (21-28), everything receding into a held G minor
(29-32). Gm-Eb-Bb-F in the verses, Eb-Bb-Cm-D at each section's turn.

## The techniques, and what each is for

- **Slow envelopes.** The pad's amplitude takes 2.6 s to arrive and 4 s to
  leave; the halo attacks over 0.45 s like a bow, not a keypress. Nothing
  synthetic starts on a downbeat.
- **Tone that moves on its own.** Each pad and halo voice carries a sub-audio
  filter LFO (0.13 Hz and 0.21 Hz), so the cutoff drifts continuously instead
  of sitting still - the static quality of a held synth is what reads as
  synthetic most immediately.
- **Velocity opens tone, not just level** (`cutoff filter ... velocity`):
  a crescendo brightens the pad the way bow pressure brightens a string
  section, and the A-section pad swells across a whole `cresc` phrase.
- **Layer inside, not beside.** The strings' sound is literally
  `sampled "vsco/strings.sfz" + gain 0.5 padS`: the pad has no part of its
  own once the strings exist; it is part of their body. The standalone pad
  appears only in bars 5-12, before there are strings to fuse with.
- **Support roles only.** The bass is sine-led with a sub octave, felt under
  the pizzicato; the halo doubles the violin tune an octave up at a tenth of
  the gain, under vibrato; filtered-noise "breaths" swell into each
  transition instead of announcing it.
- **Played, not sequenced.** Every part, kit included, is humanized; the hats
  are swung (0.58-0.6); the bass rings legato into its next note. In
  Emberfall the machines were grid-tight on purpose - here nothing is.

## Measured, not just claimed

Renders are deterministic, so muting a layer and subtracting sample-by-sample
isolates exactly what that layer contributes:

| Layer | Where | Depth under the mix |
|---|---|---|
| melody halo | the tune, bars 21-28 | -16.5 dB |
| inner pad (inside the strings sound) | the lift, bars 13-20 | -6.3 dB |
| inner pad | the tune, bars 21-28 | -5.6 dB |

The halo is a sheen you would miss, not a line you notice; the pad is about
half the strings' amplitude, but confined to low-mid warmth with no attacks,
so it reads as body. The section arc is gradual by construction:
-26.8 (piano alone) to -23.5 (pad, kick) to -20.1 (bass, hats) to -19.4 (the
tune) dB RMS, then -22.7 receding; the A section alone blooms +2.9 dB across
its sixteen beats. Peak -5.85 dBFS under the 0.8 limit.

## Where this stands

- The blend depths above are the piece's tunable contract: `gain 0.5` inside
  `ensS` and the halo's instrument gain are the two knobs, now with measured
  meanings.
- What no measurement settles is whether the pad's slow attack feels like
  breathing or like lag on the downbeats it follow - the strings carry those
  attacks by design, and that division of labour is the piece's central bet.
  It needs ears.
- The kit is still the synthesized presets; at these velocities and with
  swing they pass as a brushed kit, but a recorded kit or gentler noise-based
  presets would deepen the illusion.
- As in Emberfall, MIDI export refuses the piece (unpitched kit, no
  percussion mapping yet) and the ending's single `rit [hold]` lives in the
  piano, the one tempo writer.
